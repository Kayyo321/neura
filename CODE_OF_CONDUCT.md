# Code of conduct

This code applies to everyone who takes part in Neura: in issues, pull requests, reviews, commit messages, the wiki, and any other space the project uses, and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss the work. Criticize code, designs, and arguments, and say what would change your mind.
- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith. Ask before concluding otherwise.
- Give reviews that someone can act on: what is wrong, why it matters, and what would resolve it.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change, and that a contributor may disagree with the reasons.
- Write so that people with different backgrounds, first languages, and levels of experience can follow.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or threats, in public or in private.
- Insults or demeaning remarks about a person, including remarks about age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, race, religion, sex, or sexual orientation.
- Sexual language, imagery, or attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion, including repeating a point after it has been answered in order to wear others down.
- Retaliating against someone for reporting a problem.

## Reporting

Report a problem to a maintainer listed in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), privately, using the contact details on their GitHub profile. Say what happened, where, and when, and include links or screenshots when you have them. To report a maintainer, write to a different maintainer; when there is none, use [GitHub's abuse report](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

Do not report conduct problems in a public issue. Reports of security vulnerabilities follow the [security policy](SECURITY.md) instead.

The maintainers keep the reporter's identity and the report's contents private, except where the reporter agrees to disclosure or the law requires it.

## Enforcement

A maintainer who receives a report reads it, asks the people involved for their account where that is safe, and decides on a response in proportion to the harm and to any earlier incidents:

1. A private request to stop, with an explanation of what was unacceptable.
2. A warning that states the consequence of repeating the behavior.
3. A temporary block from the repository.
4. A permanent block from the repository.

A maintainer may also edit, hide, or delete comments, commits, and other contributions that break this code. A maintainer who is party to a report takes no part in deciding it.

The person reported is told the decision and the reason. The reporter is told the outcome.
